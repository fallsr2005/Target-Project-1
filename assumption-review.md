# Assumption Review

Dated October 20, 2026. These are review comments and proposed responses for the current model. They are not a record of a conversation or an accepted student decision. Challenger credit and actual student disposition: pending. Assistance is recorded in the required AI-use register.

## The cash balance is an estimate, not a filing fact

The model can balance perfectly and still be wrong about October cash. Inventory was building for the holiday season. Why should last year's timing describe this year's purchases and supplier payments?

Proposed response: show the August filing balance, the seasonal bridge and October estimate separately. Test a slower inventory release and tighter supplier terms before relying on excess cash. Actual student response: pending.

## Investment and depreciation need to tell the same story

Capex starts above depreciation and later declines. A flat 3% depreciation rate does not establish that the store and digital asset base can support the growth forecast. What assets are being added, how long do they last, and when do they start depreciating?

Proposed response: retain the PP&E reconciliation, disclose the missing cohort detail, and compare the proxy with reported depreciation and investment plans. Keep the historical capex flag visible. Actual student response: pending.

## Calling spending one-time does not make it disappear

The tariff refund is clearly a separately identified benefit. Transformation costs are harder: retailers often keep reorganizing. Adding all of them back could make the recurring margin look better than the business actually earns.

Proposed response: preserve the reported bridge, exclude a repeated refund from future profit, and leave recurring transformation effort inside the operating cost assumption unless specific evidence supports removal. Actual student response: pending.

## Most value is still beyond the detailed forecast

Roughly three quarters of enterprise value comes from the terminal period. That means the investment view depends heavily on sustainable margins, reinvestment and discount rates. A neat five-year forecast cannot resolve that uncertainty by itself.

Proposed response: present the WACC/g grid, combined stress range and reverse DCF beside the base case. Explain why a 12% incremental return is plausible or change it. Actual student response: pending.

## Peer premiums need an economic explanation

Costco's membership economics and Walmart's scale are different from Target's. Their higher multiples do not show that Target should trade at their median. Reported Target earnings also include the tariff refund.

Proposed response: keep both companies contextual, use consistent lease/cash conventions, show reported versus refund-adjusted Target EBIT, and do not average contextual premiums into a target price. Actual student response: pending.

## A recommendation needs an entry and an exit rule

Watch/defer is understandable at the reference price, but it needs conditions that someone can actually monitor. What evidence would make you start a position, and what would make you abandon the recovery thesis?

Proposed response: decide on an entry cushion, name the review owner, and accept or revise the comparable-sales, recurring-margin, inventory and source-failure triggers in the proposed policy. Actual student response: pending.
