## Source definitions and opening classifications

The [FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/27419/000002741926000016/tgt-20260131.htm) Note 11, p. 55, decomposes the January 31 other-current-assets total 2,213 into receivables 1,265, vendor income receivable 542, prepaid expenses 223, and other 183. Note 15, p. 57, decomposes the 6,478 accrued liabilities into wages 1,565, gift cards 1,197, other taxes 704, dividends 516, income tax 440, current operating lease 372, workers' compensation/general liability 310, interest 138, and other 1,236. These reported totals and classifications are **January**, not August balances. Note 9 reports January book overdrafts of 221 within accounts payable and 7 in accruals; amounts at August are not established and require separation before a final operating-working-capital classification.

The [August Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/27419/000002741926000042/tgt-20260801.htm) reports 2,268 other current assets and 6,738 accrued liabilities. Note 5 identifies 25 of prepaid forward contracts. Note 2 reports 959 gift card liability and 178 estimated return liability; these are included within the estimated operating accrual group, not added on top. The ROIC table p. 21 gives total current and noncurrent operating lease liabilities of 3,733; subtract reported noncurrent 3,332 to infer current 401. The full August note-by-note split is unavailable from these disclosures.

For an explicitly provisional opening bridge, the engine assigns 1,700 of August other current assets to operating assets, 25 to the identified forward contract and leaves **543 unclassified**. For accrued liabilities it assigns 4,000 to operational accruals, 440 income tax, 516 dividends and 138 interest using January placeholders, 401 to current operating leases, and leaves **1,243 unclassified**. Both bridges sum exactly to the August reported totals; the estimated amounts are not represented as actual August disclosure. The unclassified balances and identified financial/tax/lease balances are kept outside operating working capital and held constant as a simplifying assumption, not asserted to be zero or non-operating with certainty. Their cash effects and classification must be investigated before decision use. The 1.5% and 4.5% operating drivers are still proxies, not verified components.

## Depreciation and asset schedule

Note 12, p. 55, says total FY2025 depreciation, including the part in cost of sales, was approximately 3.1 billion and reports 33,749 net PP&E; the cash-flow statement reports D&A of 3,134, while income separately presents 2,617 excluding cost-of-sales depreciation. Their 517 difference is not verified as the cost-of-sales component. The model uses one **provisional** total D&A schedule based on 3% of sales, allocates an assumed 16.5% to cost of sales and the remainder to separate D&A, subtracts the total once from PP&E, and adds it once to indirect operating cash flow. This is a model convention, not a proven expense breakdown. It includes all D&A in PP&E by assumption although amortization of other assets is not reconciled; asset lives, land/construction in progress, disposal and finance lease additions need further analysis. The nonnegative-PP&E test does not prove an asset-life schedule is feasible.

## Timeline, seasonal treatment, and research changes

The opening is the August 1 reported balance sheet. Reported H1 sales, expense and capex are used as a boundary; August 2–October 19 is **estimated elapsed activity**, and only October 20–January 30 is the future FY2026 stub. The remaining-year sales and other flows are allocated 45% to the elapsed period and 55% to the stub; the cash balances at cutoff are therefore estimates, not reported October values. This is an unvalidated simplification that does not establish actual seasonal quarters. Inventories and payables move partway from August balances toward estimated year-end targets during the elapsed period; no later disclosure is used.

FY2027–FY2031 follow as five full fiscal years. Annual FY2028 is 371 days (53 weeks), and its assumed 3% is now explicitly a **52-week-equivalent** growth assumption; the 53rd week is added separately as one average week. FY2029 grows from the FY2028 52-week-equivalent base, so that additional week does not become permanent underlying growth. This is a meaningful clarification of Step 5's annual growth input; the extra week’s size is an assumption, not a validated seasonal observation. Period dates and the extra stub are project choices, subject to course-instruction confirmation.

## Debt, leases, tax and distributions

The FY2025 10-K Note 17 reports principal by maturity year, including 2,000 in 2026, 97 in 2027, 581 in 2028, 1,000 in 2029 and 1,230 in 2030. The Q2 10-Q Note 8 says 1,000 of the 2026 amount was repaid in April. The draft assigns the remaining 1,000 to the post-cutoff FY2026 stub as a timing **assumption**, not a verified October event. It assumes the identified FY2027–FY2030 amounts are paid in those periods. The 2031 portion of the filing's thereafter bucket is **unknown**: a temporary zero scheduled payment is used, with a warning; obtain the individual maturities before relying on FY2031.

The 10-K reports bond cohorts by maturity range and weighted-average coupon rates of 2.9%, 4.9%, 6.3%, 4.0%, 3.8% and 3.9%. The model approximates August cohort carrying amounts using the January groups and April repayment; it transfers 10 between the first two carrying buckets to reconcile the rounded nominal maturity schedule. This is an explicit approximation, not a reported transaction. It uses those group rates for existing bonds, not the new-debt rate. The residual 1,953 of reported debt includes finance leases and swap/carrying adjustments; it is held constant with a 4% illustrative charge informed by FY2025 finance-lease information. Future finance-lease principal, new lease additions, actual rate swaps and current/noncurrent reclassification are not separately projected; financial statements show total debt, not a fully resolved current/noncurrent maturity split. These gaps are material.

The new facility rate 5% applies **only to new draws**. If operating/investing cash, existing maturities and the assumed distribution would leave cash under the 1,000 reserve, the model draws up to the 4,000 facility limit, solving for incremental half-period interest and tax. If capacity is insufficient, it records a failed liquidity check and cash shortfall rather than inserting unexplained financing. Dividends are not automatically cancelled; a shortfall signals that the policy is infeasible. Repurchases are assumed zero in this draft. The facility expires in August 2031; the engine assumes existing draws are repaid during FY2031 and no new facility is available in that fiscal year, with interest simplified over the full period. Actual expiry refinancing and timing need investigation. Existing dividends are not presented as approved future management policy.

Target declared 1.16 per share payable September 1, 2026 ([June 11 release](https://corporate.target.com/press/release/2026/06/target-corporation-increases-quarterly-dividend-by-1-8-percent)), and separately declared 1.16 payable December 1 ([September 23 release](https://corporate.target.com/press/release/2026/09/target-corporation-declares-regular-quarterly-dividend)). The engine estimates the dollar cash payment using August shares, not a verified record-date entitlement. Later quarterly dividends at the same amount are assumptions. Dividend payable is held constant and each modeled period assumes declaration equals payment; this is an accounting simplification, especially across year-end, not proof of future declarations. Interest payable and income tax payable are similarly held fixed; taxes are modeled at 24% of positive pretax earnings with no immediate loss tax benefit. Other noncurrent balances and operating lease assets/liabilities are held constant; actual changes and related cash flows are unknown.

## Normalization and check results

The settlement, transformation charges and tariff benefit are described separately in the Step 4 research; no historical cash is erased. The H1 reported tariff benefit is removed from the recurring expense comparison once; the reported August cash remains unchanged. Forecast SG&A is assumed to contain recurring transformation spending, with no separate event-specific future charge; this is an assumption and may omit material costs. The 4,774 rounded FY2025 bridge versus Target's 4,775 is retained and tested with a 1 million tolerance. Accounting identities use 0.000001 million tolerance; this is a computational tolerance, not a source-accuracy guarantee.
