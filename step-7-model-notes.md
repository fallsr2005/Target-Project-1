## FCFF and reconciliation

For each future stub/full year, operating tax is 24% of positive operating income; no immediate tax benefit is assumed for losses. FCFF = operating income − operating tax + total operating D&A − cash capex − increase in operating working capital. The operating working-capital change is the inverse of the working-capital cash-flow component from the Step 6 statement, consisting only of the modeled operating inventory, payables, other operating assets and operating accruals. It excludes financing balances, cash, tax, dividends and separately handled leases. Because the classifications are provisional and some components are held fixed, the result inherits their limitations.

The Step 6 indirect operating cash flow begins with net income and adds D&A and the operating working-capital movement. For the same period the engine checks: **FCFF = operating cash flow + interest expense + reported-model tax − operating tax − capex**. This restores financing interest and replaces the model's leveraged income tax with unlevered operating tax. No dividends, repurchases, borrowing, debt principal or financing cash flow enter FCFF. D&A appears once as an operating expense in EBIT and once as a noncash addback; the same total is allocated between cost of sales and separately presented expense once. The simplified model has no other noncash adjustments or variable tax-payable changes; if those schedules change, update this reconciliation rather than assume they vanish.

## Cost of capital and discounting

The WACC calculation and source qualifications are in `step-7-source-ledger.md`. The inputs produce 9.29% cost of equity and 8.344% WACC from the specified CAPM and assumed capital weights. The 1.00 beta, 6% marginal debt cost, 80/20 capital weights and 24% tax shield are provisional; the risk-free input is an October 16 Treasury observation. The October 1 implied ERP's precise publication timestamp is not archived. The Step 6 coupon-based interest schedule is not used as the WACC borrowing rate. The assumed WACC is neither Edition A's 9% nor a verified market WACC; both are hypotheses.

Treat each FCFF as received at the respective fiscal year-end. The exponent is calendar days between October 19 and that end divided by 365. The stub ends January 30, 2027, 103 days after the cutoff; FY2031 ends January 31, 2032. There is no discounted cash flow for the August–October elapsed estimate. This end-of-day convention approximates rather than models intraday timings and is applied to all cases.

## Terminal economics

At the FY2031 end, assume sustainable NOPAT grows by 2% the next year and incremental invested capital earns 12% after tax. Next-year reinvestment is next-year NOPAT × 2% / 12%; terminal FCFF is the remaining NOPAT. Terminal value is that next FCFF divided by WACC minus growth and discounted from the FY2031 end. Thus growth requires explicit reinvestment rather than mechanically growing the last FCFF. The 12% return is a provisional marginal-return assumption, not the company's verified historical ROIC, and the post-forecast asset, maintenance-capex and working-capital composition is not separately articulated. Growth and returns are assumed perpetually constant; require WACC > growth ≥ 0 and incremental return > growth. If incremental return is below WACC, raising growth can reduce value; this outcome is tested and not forced away. Review whether 12% is defensible, and whether this terminal steady state fits the forecast.

## Bridge and scenarios

The bridge uses the **same base estimated October balance** for all cases: estimated cash less the provisional operating cash reserve is added once; estimated debt including finance leases is deducted once. Cash retained for operations is already excluded from the addition and is not deducted again. It does not use any forecast year-end cash in today's bridge. Under the chosen operating-lease convention rent remains in operating cost/FCFF and operating lease liabilities are not separately subtracted. WACC and future peer multiples must be consistent with that convention. Shares are August issued common shares plus a 2.2 million provisional proxy derived from the Q2 EPS weighted-average difference, not a verified cutoff dilution estimate.

Other claims and non-operating assets, including mixed unclassified balances and any relevant pension claims/assets, are **unknown**. The calculation leaves their signed adjustment null and labels the reported equity and per-share values partial; it does not establish their combined adjustment as zero. Review the note disclosures and classify without double-counting liabilities already in operating working capital or debt. Book debt is a proxy for market debt and valuation-date estimates depend on the uncertain elapsed roll-forward.

The low scenario reduces FY2027–FY2031 sales growth one percentage point while increasing merchandise cost by 0.8 percentage point and SG&A by 0.3. The high scenario increases growth one point, reduces merchandise cost 0.8 and SG&A 0.3 points, and raises capex 0.5 point to support growth. Base uses Step 6; all three keep the current-year estimate, stub, cutoff bridge, WACC and terminal inputs unchanged. These case paths do not express probabilities. Each case reruns the statement engine; any financing feasibility failure is separately visible. The WACC/growth grid changes one pair of terminal parameters at a time, holding the operating path and 12% incremental return constant. It is not a scenario probability distribution.

The reverse function solves terminal growth over a stated interval with all other inputs fixed. It refuses to report an observed market result without a positive, dated, source-linked official close explicitly accepted by the student; the default price is null, so reverse DCF and upside/downside remain pending. A solution is conditional on the assumed terminal method and incomplete bridge and is not a unique explanation of a market price.
