## Classification before looking at multiples

Document each candidate's eligibility with original-source locators and observations before collecting or displaying that candidate's multiple. Record the date of that classification and retain exclusions, not just chosen names. Do not change the classification after observing multiples without a separately dated, evidence-based amendment and an explicit record of what was already seen.

| Dimension | Core valuation peer criteria and interpretation | Contextual comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Business and merchandise | Primarily consumer-facing merchandise retail, with a broad multi-category assortment and meaningful overlap in essentials and discretionary goods; verify revenue mix and whether the public company's economics are predominantly retail. A narrow specialist, wholesale club, marketplace, or grocery-heavy model is not automatically interchangeable. Explain gross-margin effects of mix and private brands. | A narrower retailer, predominantly grocery, membership-funded, marketplace or other business may inform a specific operating driver, but not the core multiple range when its economics materially differ. |
| Geography and customers | Prefer a predominantly U.S.-exposed business selling to households under comparable consumer, tax, labor and competitive conditions. Similarity is economic, not identical branding or presence of every feature. | Businesses with substantially different sources of profit can inform channel or advertising context but should not drive the core range. |
| Scale and operating economics | Compare sales scale, operating and gross margins, growth, return and cyclicality; examine capex, owned versus leased stores, inventory turnover, payables, and working-capital seasonality. Similarity in revenue alone is insufficient. No invented hard numerical cutoff: record observed gaps and why they do or do not materially impair comparison before viewing multiples. | Significant scale, margin, capital-intensity or working-capital differences require contextual treatment if their effects cannot be reasonably explained. |
| Reporting, period and evidence | Require cutoff-available audited annual statements and enough interim information to construct a comparable trailing period, consistent accounting/lease definitions and traceable price, shares and claims. Core status requires acceptable evidence on all dimensions, not a mechanical scoring threshold. Identify material tradeoffs and do not treat differences in margins, ownership, growth or risk as valuation errors. Contextual observations are separately labeled and never blended into a core range as if equivalent. If no company passes, say so; no peer count is mandated. Candidate business data should precede valuation data, though full prevention of prior familiarity cannot be proven; disclose any prior knowledge that could bias judgment.

## Point-in-time and period conventions

The proposed market observation is the unadjusted regular-session October 19, 2026 close at 4:00 p.m. Eastern for each listed common security. Verify market calendar, source, timestamp, split/corporate-action basis and official-close status separately. A foreign market's different close requires a documented same-cutoff convention and FX as of or before cutoff; if not defensible, exclude from core numeric comparison. Record publication, financial-period end, market timestamp and retrieval date independently. No information first published after cutoff may be introduced into this historical comparison.

Use latest available trailing twelve months (TTM), calculated as latest filed annual plus current-year-to-date less matching prior-year-to-date, using consistent definitions and avoiding overlap. Record actual start/end dates, weeks, and source equations for each company. Target's latest eligible interim ends August 1, 2026; peer latest available periods may differ. Prefer broadly aligned ends and comparable seasonal coverage; explicitly show staleness and material mismatches. For a 53-week period, use a disclosed 52-week adjusted measure only when the extra week's effect can be sourced or reasonably isolated with a transparent bridge. Otherwise show the reported period and qualify or exclude where the extra week materially distorts comparison; do not mechanically divide by 53 when seasonal week economics differ. Never splice incompatible periods or use later results to fill gaps.

## Proposed multiples and bridge definitions

Use USD millions for enterprise and equity values and earnings, million shares, and USD per share. Record source currency and as-of FX where needed. Present reported results and an independently documented normalized alternative side by side; every normalization needs amount, sign, source, affected line, tax treatment, recurrence rationale and consistency across firms. Do not automatically reverse restructuring or transformation costs. Handle the Target settlement gain and tariff refund per the existing normalization evidence, preserving actual cash. A normalized result is unavailable if adjustments cannot be bridged.

**Enterprise multiple: EV / EBITDA.** Numerator is cutoff market value of common equity (verified unadjusted close × contemporaneous diluted valuation shares, with options/convertibles treated consistently) plus interest-bearing debt, including finance lease liabilities exactly once, plus fair value of preferred equity, noncontrolling interests and other material non-common claims, less eligible excess unrestricted cash and identified non-operating assets not already captured elsewhere. Market debt is preferred; a sourced book-debt proxy with as-of date, material rate/price differences and uncertainty is permissible only when market value is unavailable. Restricted cash and the documented operating-required cash reserve are not deducted as excess cash; the reserve is not subtracted a second time. The same treatment and an explicit signed bridge apply across companies and when translating an EV-based Target indication to equity value. Reconcile other claims and holdings instead of assuming a missing amount is zero. Use current diluted shares and instrument-specific treasury-stock or conversion methods where supportable; avoid counting a claim in both dilution and debt or preferred equity. Flag estimated cutoff cash/debt versus reported balance dates.

Denominator is TTM operating profit before interest and income tax plus **total operating** depreciation and amortization included in that profit, counted once, with discontinued/non-operating items and inclusions identified. Reconcile the D&A to financial statement and note presentation; cash-flow D&A cannot automatically be treated as operating D&A without reconciliation. Operating lease rent remains in operating expense and EBITDA; do not add operating lease liabilities to EV. Finance leases remain in debt and finance-lease depreciation belongs in D&A only once. If an IFRS reporter capitalizes operating leases, rebuild a consistent pre-lease-adjusted rent-in-EBITDA and operating-lease-excluded EV using disclosed figures, including income statement effects; otherwise exclude its EV/EBITDA from the core numeric comparison. Do not treat an unreconciled lease-adjusted EBITDA as comparable. EBITDA excludes reinvestment and working capital, so use capex, store ownership and working-capital differences to qualify interpretation.

**Equity multiple: P/E.** Numerator is the same cutoff unadjusted common-share price; denominator is TTM diluted earnings per common share from continuing operations attributable to common shareholders, with declared treatment of exceptional items and tax effects. Construct TTM earnings and weighted-average diluted share basis from annual/current/prior interim data when supportable, rather than simply adding inconsistent EPS figures; reconcile with reported EPS and explain approximations. For a normalized P/E, use normalized earnings attributable to common divided by the period's reconciled diluted weighted-average shares. Historical diluted EPS shares are period averages, not cutoff diluted shares. Price/EPS and market-cap/current-share-count divided by period earnings may differ; do not claim they are algebraically identical without reconciling share bases. Debt, cash, lease expenses and non-common interests affect earnings and equity price rather than being added again to P/E; check consistency and exclude unreconciled unusual capital structures. When applying an observed P/E, multiply the stated Target EPS basis to produce a per-share indication, without applying an EV bridge again.

If EBITDA, earnings, or EPS is zero or negative, mark the corresponding multiple not meaningful and omit it from the numeric core range; retain the company and reason in the evidence table. Tiny positive denominators and extreme observations require economic investigation and a disclosed treatment established without choosing results to fit a desired conclusion. Missing price, diluted shares, claims, lease reconciliation or period data makes the affected result pending, not zero. An incomplete EV bridge cannot support a complete equity value.

## Exclusions, exceptions, and aggregation

Exclude a candidate from a core numerical multiple for a materially different business model, missing cutoff-eligible primary evidence, unreconciled accounting/lease treatment, noncomparable financial period, unknown material claims or unusable denominator. A company may be contextual for one question or eligible for one multiple but not another. Exceptional items require separate reported and supported normalized bridges; unexplained adjustments do not qualify as normalized. Do not compensate for a small peer set by relaxing standards after observing multiples.

An exception requires evidence, a stated reason why remaining differences are immaterial or a transparent quantified reconciliation, timestamp, reviewer/student decision, and a prominent disclosure of the limitation; unsupported exceptions remain pending. Evaluate each core multiple individually and show the range/distribution only when evidence is adequate; no mandatory peer count, automatic outlier deletion or mechanical average with DCF. Explain differences using margins, mix, growth, capital intensity, risk, accounting, periods, bridge and evidence quality.

## Transaction evidence

Use a transaction only if a comparable retail business and transaction date before cutoff are supported by primary deal disclosures, deal terms and target financials available by cutoff. Record announcement and close dates, target, acquired stake, currency, consideration, debt assumed, cash and other claims, and distinguish announcement-date implied enterprise value from equity consideration. Investigate control premium, bid conditions, expected synergies and buyer-specific strategy, business mix, carve-out/standalone financials and denominator timing. A control price incorporating synergies is not directly comparable to a minority public-share trading multiple; quantify supported adjustments or explain why comparison is qualitative only. Omit unsuitable transactions and record the search/evidence gap; a transaction is not mandatory merely to fill a row.
